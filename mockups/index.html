Reference analysis

What the references are doing.

The visual language is a premium creative marketplace, not a cyberpunk dashboard. The protocol should borrow the confidence and editorial calm, not the marketplace content.

Reference 1 · multi-screen marketplace

A complete product suite: home, talent browse, project create, filters, and category pages. The consistency comes from a single paper tone, thin rules, serif display headings, and a compact green CTA.

  • LayoutTop nav, asymmetric split hero, photography-led panels, generous spacing, wide modal with a left stepper.
  • TypeEditorial serif for headlines, geometric sans for UI labels, uppercase micro-copy, tight display leading.
  • ColorIvory canvas, white cards, near-black ink, forest green primary, sage/olive imagery, warm wood and terracotta accents.
  • RadiiCards around 8–12px; buttons 4–6px; pills only for status/filter chips; no glassmorphism.
  • VibeHuman, premium, trustworthy, editorial, calm. The opposite of a dark terminal.

Reference 2 · brand collage

A brand-level composition that makes the system feel established: oversized serif statement, layered product screens, warm photography, fine grid lines, and small uppercase labels.

  • TypeLarge all-caps serif statement; smaller sans uppercase subtitle; restrained tracking and high contrast.
  • TextureFaint grid on the canvas, warm paper background, subtle shadows, no gradients as decoration.
  • BrandSmall star mark, serif wordmark, forest-green pill CTA, cream text, understated confidence.
  • RhythmBig statement → product collage → large evidence numbers → small proof points. Generous vertical breathing room.
  • VibeCreative studio / editorial marketplace. Human and premium, with technical product screens embedded.
